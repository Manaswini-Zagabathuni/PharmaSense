# FAERS/AEMS scientific limitations

PharmaSense treats disproportionality findings as **potential reporting signals**, never proof of
causation, incidence, prevalence, event frequency, or comparative drug safety.

FDA describes spontaneous reports as potentially incomplete, inaccurate, delayed, duplicated,
and unverified. Public data does not include every field available to FDA reviewers, such as case
narratives. The system also lacks a reliable exposure denominator.

Primary FDA references:

- [FDA FAERS reporting and review limitations](https://www.fda.gov/media/165667/download)
- [FDA discussion of public postmarket surveillance data](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/cder-conversations/understanding-cders-postmarket-safety-surveillance-programs-and-public-data)

## Major sources of bias and uncertainty

- Underreporting and selective reporting
- Stimulated and notoriety reporting after publicity or regulatory action
- Duplicate submissions and later follow-up versions
- Confounding by indication, disease severity, and concomitant drugs
- Uncertain product role and temporal relationship
- Missing, inconsistent, or invalid demographic and event dates
- Brand, ingredient, spelling, and combination-product ambiguity
- Terminology and coding changes over time
- Multiple testing and instability from small counts
- Regulatory actions based on evidence beyond public adverse-event reports

## Required interpretation controls

Future analysis must use version-aware deduplication, show underlying contingency counts, enforce
minimum-count rules, quantify uncertainty, address multiple comparisons, preserve time cutoffs,
and make normalization decisions auditable. Historical evaluation may only use data available
before the evaluated action. A missing FDA action is not automatically a false-positive signal.

## Intended use

PharmaSense is an educational and research platform. It is not validated for diagnosis,
treatment, prescribing, patient-specific risk estimation, or regulatory decision-making. Users
must consult qualified healthcare professionals and official current labeling for medical
decisions.
